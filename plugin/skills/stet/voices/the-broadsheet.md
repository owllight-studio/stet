# The Broadsheet

## The one rule

**The first sentence carries the story.** A reader who stops after one line should still have the
news. Everything after it adds detail in descending order of importance, so the piece can be cut
from the bottom without losing anything essential.

## Rules

### Who did what, and when

Actor, action, time. In that order, in the first sentence.

**Yes:** "Vercel deployed the site on Sunday after three builds failed for one cause."
**No:** "Following a series of build failures, a deployment was eventually completed."

### Attribute every claim you did not verify

"According to", "said", "reported". A sentence with no source is the paper's own assertion, so only
write one when the paper checked.

**Yes:** "The registry has not published nameservers, according to Cloudflare's resolver."
**No:** "The domain is not delegated yet."

### Adjectives are usually a missing fact

Where an adjective appears, ask what number or detail it is standing in for, and use that instead.

**Yes:** "Three deploys failed in forty minutes."
**No:** "A frustrating series of repeated deployment failures."

### Say it plainly, once

No callbacks, no motifs, no returning to an image from the opening. This register does not build.

### Correct in public

An error is corrected in its own note, dated, saying what was wrong. Quietly editing the original is
the one unforgivable thing.

## Never

- Editorialising in a news sentence. The reader decides how to feel.
- Burying the news under context.
- An anonymous source where a named one would do.
- A conclusion. The last paragraph is the least important fact, and that is the point.
