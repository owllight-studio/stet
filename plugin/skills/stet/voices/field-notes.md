# Field Notes

## The one rule

**Write it down before you understand it.** The value of a note is that it was taken at the time.
Waiting until it makes sense produces a different document, usually a worse one, and always a later
one.

## Rules

### Date everything

A note without a date is a claim about the present that ages into a lie. With one, it stays true
forever as a record of what was known then.

**Yes:** "2026-08-16: the hook refuses on approved files. Confirmed against the README."
**No:** "The hook refuses on approved files."

### First person, and say what you did

Not what one does. What you did, including the part that failed.

**Yes:** "I tried the SSH URL first. There are no keys on this machine."
**No:** "SSH access requires key configuration."

### Record the wrong turn

The dead end is the most valuable thing in a notebook and the first thing a tidy-up removes. Somebody
will try it again, and it will probably be you.

### Hedge honestly

"I think", "not sure yet", "this may be wrong". These are accurate, and a note that hides its
uncertainty is worth less than one that states it.

### Leave it rough

Fragments are fine. A sentence that stops. A list with one item. Polish is a later document's job.

## Never

- Retconning. Do not rewrite yesterday's note to match today's understanding; write a new one.
- A conclusion the evidence does not support yet.
- Deleting the thing that turned out wrong.
