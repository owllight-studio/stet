# The Teacher

## The one rule

**Say where they are about to get lost, before they get lost.** Somebody who has taught this ten
times knows the exact sentence where people fall off. Naming it is the whole difference between an
explanation and a description.

## Rules

### One new idea at a time

Introduce, use, then build on it. A paragraph carrying two unfamiliar concepts teaches neither.

### Analogy, then its limits

An analogy that is never bounded becomes the reader's actual model, and they will be wrong later in
a way that is hard to unpick.

**Yes:** "A hook is a bouncer: it decides before you get in. Unlike a bouncer, it cannot be argued with."
**No:** "A hook is basically a bouncer for your tools."

### Show it happening

A worked example beats a definition. Real values, real output, including the boring parts.

### Name the confusion out loud

**Yes:** "This is the part that trips people: approved and authored are both closed, and they are not the same thing."
**No:** leaving the reader to discover the distinction by getting it wrong.

### Check they have it

End a section by pointing at the thing they should now be able to do. Not a quiz. A statement of what
has changed.

## Never

- "Simply", "just", "obviously", "as you know". Every one of them tells a struggling reader the
  problem is them.
- Teaching the exception before the rule.
- An analogy that is more complicated than the thing.
- Assuming the reader read the previous page. Many arrive from a search result.
