Nobody wrote this.

You can spot machine-written prose in a paragraph. Sometimes in a line. A cover letter, a property listing, a poem somebody sent their mother, all arriving in the same voice. Every sentence the same length. Every list with a little paragraph in front of it announcing that a list is coming.

Stet is a plugin for Claude Code, and it does two things about that. Seventeen voices come with it, and none is a mood you typed into a box. Each was read off named books before it was written: Chandler for the noir, Darwin's Beagle notebooks for the field notes, the Reuters handbook for the wire. Then counted, sentence by sentence. Take one as it stands, edit it, or describe what you are after.

The second thing is that you can lock a line. A slogan, a testimonial, the sentence you spent an hour on. Everything around that line can be pulled apart and rebuilt twice before lunch, and the line comes back exactly as you left it.

Line E · Establish

Seventeen voices, and the reading behind them

Why not just tell the agent how you want to sound? Because what you can tell it is an adjective. Friendly but professional. Clear and concise. Nobody can check an adjective, so nobody does.

These were built the other way round, off named books rather than out of anybody's impression of them. Fifteen of Darwin's Beagle notebooks. Chandler, Hammett and Cain. Ninety-three pages of Kubernetes documentation. Every file says which.

What comes back is a set of numbers. How long the sentences run. Where the pauses land. How often the writing hedges. Each one sits in the file beside the book it was counted off, so when a draft stops sounding like you, you can point at the number that moved.

And if you cannot name the voice you want? Most people cannot. Describe it in your own words, badly, and the agents go and do the reading. What comes back is yours.

Line R · Refine

The tells, named and checked

An em dash. “Not X, it is Y.” A small paragraph standing in front of every list to announce that a list is coming. A closing sentence summarising the paragraph you just read.

Every voice file ends by naming how a fake of it gives itself away, and across seventeen that comes to 102 separate ways. Put the 102 together and you have a list of what a machine writes.

Can a checker catch a voice? No, and tells does not try. It catches the phrases instead, because you can count an em dash and you cannot count a voice. No dependencies, no network, no model, and it ran over this page.

Line A · Authorship

The line that has to stand

A slogan. A testimonial. A promise your lawyers went over twice. The sentence you spent an hour on, threw away, and wrote back almost exactly as it was.

Lock it, and the agent can change the voice, move the section and rebuild the page around it. The line does not move.

Stet holds it as your words, character for character, rather than as a line number. That is how it survives the page being torn up around it. Change the words yourself and the lock comes off, because they are different words.

A hook reads that record before the edit lands. It does not advise. It refuses.

Line V · Evaluate

The page still has to be true

A price on your site went up three weeks ago and the page still quotes the old one. A study you cited was retracted in March. Your footnote has no idea. Two percentages in the same paragraph do not add up to the total sitting underneath them.

None of that is a matter of taste. It is arithmetic. Ten checks run in your build, and a build that fails is a page that does not ship.

Line O · Operate

Half of it does not need a model at all

The CLI · 25 commands

Questions with one answer

Is this word spelled two ways across your project. Does this figure still match the command that produced it. Was this paper retracted.

Nobody has to read anything to answer those. They run in your build with nothing installed.

The plugin · 7 readings, 10 agents

Questions that need reading

Does this source actually support the sentence citing it. Can a reader who is not you follow this paragraph. What voice is this person reaching for.

Each of those needs somebody to read and decide, so they live where there is a model. The help screen prints them under a heading saying they are not there.

So how much of it costs you model time? Seven commands. The other twenty-nine never load one, and twenty-five of those ship as a CLI you can run in a build with nothing installed.
