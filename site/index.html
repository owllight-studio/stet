The problem

Point an agent at your writing. Three things go wrong.

It writes too much

Every sentence turns up with half another sentence stapled to it, and no list arrives without a small paragraph standing in front of it to announce that a list is coming. So you cut. Unwinding it takes longer than writing the thing yourself would have taken, and the hours come out of the one job nobody can do in your place.

It rewrites what is not its own

It goes into paragraphs that were never its business. You worked that one over and it comes back improved. Nobody asked. You asked for a redesign, or a fix in some file three doors down, and your words were standing too close to the door.

It leaves what it should refresh

A sentence quotes a number that moved three weeks ago. Nothing in the file says where that number came from, so nothing noticed. The page says it anyway, with a straight face.

The model

Every piece of writing carries a state. Approval is what makes it yours.

A draft belongs to the agent until somebody reads it and takes it. Nothing else hands it over. Not time. Not the writing being good. Not the agent having written every word.

draft
An agent wrote it and nobody has approved it. An agent may rewrite it freely.
approved
An agent wrote it and a person accepted it. Closed. Rebuilt only if asked, each time.
authored
A person wrote it. Closed, and never regenerated at all. Only its author replaces it.

Ownership runs per sentence, not per file. Fix one line on the proof sheet and that line is yours. The paragraph around it stays open. Sentences are held by their own words rather than by where they sit, so yours survive everything around them moving, and they lapse the moment the words change. You own what you wrote. Nothing more, and nothing less.

Policy sits alongside it and says what may still be done to closed writing. authored plus refresh is the pair the whole thing exists to make sayable. These are my words and I want the numbers in them kept true. Change the figure. Leave the sentence. 

---
stet:
  state: authored
  policy: refresh
  sources: [corpus.rampShare]
---

Enforcement

A hook that refuses. Not a rule that asks.

Every other part of this is an instruction, and instructions are advice. Advice loses. A PreToolUse hook reads the state before any edit lands and refuses the ones that were never the agent's to make. 

VOICE.md is authored. You may not edit it.

A person wrote this. Do not edit it and do not regenerate it.

You may propose a rewrite in your reply. Show it to the author.
Do not put it in the file.

Three boundaries keep it out of your way. No config means the project is not using Stet, so the hook has no opinion. Only declared paths count, so code goes straight through. A file that does not exist yet is new, which is a draft, which is fine.

Voice

The other half. Writing that does not read as though a machine wrote it.

Ownership stops your words being changed. It does nothing about the words an agent adds, and that is the half readers actually notice. Tell somebody a headline was written by AI and they rate it less accurate and are less willing to pass it on. That holds whether the headline was true and whether a machine wrote it at all. The penalty lands on how the writing reads. So prose that merely sounds generated pays it, with no label on it and nobody to appeal to.

So Stet ships a library of registers. Every one of them is a machine for producing a particular feeling, and every one says how that feeling gets onto the page rather than naming it and stopping. Noir never lets the narrator say he is frightened. It says he could not hear his own footsteps. Bourdain piles up the cruel words and lets them collapse into something kind inside the same sentence, so the contempt is always the setup and never the ending. Warmth in a technical explanation turns out to be one rule: describe the material, never the reader's mind.

What the library will not do is hand you an adjective. "Warm" and "confident" cannot be followed and cannot be broken, so they are not rules. Somebody went and counted the actual books instead. Orwell breaks modern plain-English guidance inside his own manifesto, at a mean sentence of 25.9 words. Chandler's famous simile turns up once every 545 words, roughly forty times rarer than the people copying him run it. Tolkien writes shorter sentences than Fitzgerald. You do not get any of that by sitting there describing the style to yourself.

Four ways to have one. Pick a preset. Describe your own in a sentence, feeling included. Point it at writing you already did. Or let it read the project and work out the house voice from what is there.

Explore the library

Commands

Set the project up. Then work in it.

For a project that already has writing in it, the order is ingest, init, voice, ia. Read it before you describe it.

ingest

Reads everything already there, claims all of it for you, and tells you what it found. What exists, how it sounds, and every claim in it that will go stale.

init

What this is, who reads it, and what it must never be. Nobody volunteers that last one. It is the answer worth most.

voice

Works the house voice out of writing you already did, because nobody can describe their own. Rules with your own sentences as the examples, and a counter-example for each one.

ia

What exists, what job each page does, and where the next one goes. Without that last part every new page is a fresh argument.

claim · release · approve

Whose words these are. Only the author approves; the agent records it.

audit · verify · refresh

Stale claims, figures somebody typed by hand, orphaned pages, voice breaks. Then it checks each claim against its actual source and brings current only what policy allows.

Install

Early, and honest about it.

/plugin marketplace add owllight-studio/stet
/plugin install stet@stet

The Establish group, the authorship model and the hook are built. The rest is designed and not written. Stet's own writing sits at draft rather than approved, because its author has directed it and has not yet read it line by line. Marking it approved would be the exact failure the whole thing exists to stop.
