Thirty-six commands. Seven lines.

Stet is a plugin for Claude Code. It keeps agents off the sentences you wrote and keeps every figure true to the command that produced it.

Work out what the project is. Write to it. Say whose the words are. Hold it against its sources. Cut it down. Keep it current.

Those six sentences are the six coloured lines below, in that order. The seventh is the one you start every session on. Which one do you need? Whichever of those six describes your afternoon.

stet context, run on this repository 
STET  a site, 69 files, 101,071 words

WHAT YOU MAY EDIT
  67 open, 2 closed.
  Ask before editing anything closed.
  Do not release it yourself.

Why first? Because the hook will refuse an edit you had no way to see coming. One call tells you which files are closed, what the project sounds like, and what to do next.

Interchange

Ten agents

So why not just ask the agent you already have? Because two jobs do not fit in a conversation. Reading your whole back catalogue is too big for one. Deciding whether your page works is too close to it, since the thing being judged is the thing that wrote it.

AgentWhy not do it here
voice-builderMost people cannot name the voice they want. Ask them to describe it and you get adjectives
voice-researcherTen presets written from instinct had their central mechanic backwards. An impression of a voice is reliably an inversion of it
sample-readerA whole back catalogue, read once, reported in a page
corpus-readerForty thousand words of somebody else's site, read once
style-sheetThe error hides in the string a reader skips. A machine does not skip it
citation-checkerWhether a source exists is arithmetic. Whether it supports the sentence is not
source-integrityWhether a source survives is arithmetic. Whether the claim survives it changing is not
continuityNo single reading holds four hundred pages, and the contradiction is never in one place
fact-checkerAdversarial by design. A checker that wrote the claim finds it convincing
criticFresh eyes, outside the attention gravity of having written it
Terminus

Not only for marketing pages

A novel. A research paper. A screenplay, a documentation tree, an album's worth of lyrics. None of that is a marketing page, and if you write one of them your first question is whether any of this was built with you in mind.

Some checks only make sense for a website, so you tell Stet what you are writing with kinds and those switch themselves off. What is left works the same on all of them.

A line of your lyric and a line of your marketing copy are both somebody's words, and whether an agent may rewrite them has one answer.

The voice measurements are the part worth arguing about, and the honest answer is that they were counted off the register you pick. Choose Noir and you are held to Chandler and Hammett. Choose The Pitch and you are held to sixteen software home pages. Neither number means anything on the other one, which is why there are seventeen.
